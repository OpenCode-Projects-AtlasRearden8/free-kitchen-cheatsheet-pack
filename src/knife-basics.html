Knife Basics One-Pager

Grip it, cut it, keep it sharp — safer prep in one page

The Grip

  • Pinch grip: curl three fingers around the blade right where handle meets blade; thumb and index finger hold the flat sides.
  • Guide hand: your other hand holds the food — make a "claw" with knuckles forward and fingertips tucked back behind them. The blade rests lightly against the knuckles.
  • Board grip: damp towel or paper towel under the cutting board so it cannot slide. One second, saves trips to the ER.
  • Visibility rule: if you can't see your fingertips past your knuckles, curl them more.

The Rocking Motion (for a chef's knife)

  • Let the tip rest on the board while you rock the heel of the blade down and forward — smooth lurching arc, like a rocking chair.
  • Work in a rhythm, not force: sharp knives need pressure, dull ones need muscle — and they are the scary ones.
  • Move food with the flat side of the blade or a scraper — never with an open edge.

A Fast Onion Demo (mind the claw)

  1. Halve root-to-tip; peel both halves.
  2. Set flat side down on board (this is the safe no-roll step people forget).
  3. Horizontal cuts toward (but not through) the root.
  4. Vertical slices root-to-tip; then cross for dice.
  5. Sweep into a bowl or skillet — done. Wash hands and board to clear the onion smell.

Sharpness & Care

TopicWhat to do
Test sharpnessIt should glide through tomato skin without pressing. If it slides, hone first.
Hone5 strokes each side on a honing steel, weekly. Straightens the edge between sharpening.
SharpenWhetstone or pull-through sharpener, 2–3 times a year. A $2 sharpener that works beats an expensive dull one.
Wash & dryHand wash, towel-dry immediately. Never goes into the sink of soapy water (blind cuts) and never the dishwasher.
StoreMagnetic strip, block, or blade guard. The drawer-of-loose-blades is how cuts happen.
Fall ruleNever try to catch a falling knife. Step back, let it land, then pick it up by the handle.
Starter kit (~$40): one 8-inch chef's knife (a solid mid-range brand beats a block set), one paring knife, one honing steel, one cutting board. Skip the 14-piece kit — two sharp knives cook anything.
